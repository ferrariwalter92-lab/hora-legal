Privacy Policy – HORA

Last updated: 5 November 2026

HORA is an app that lets you know when your friends are free. This page explains which data we use, why, who can see it and what your rights are.

Who we are

The data controller is Walter Ferrari, Viale Tibaldi 14, 20136 Milano, Italy.

For anything about privacy, write to privacy@hora-app.com.

What data we use, why, and who sees it

  • Phone number. To sign you in with a code sent by SMS. Only you see it. A friend sees it only after a mutual “It's Hora!” and only if you chose to share it.
  • Name (for example “Giulia Rossi”). So your friends recognise you. All registered users can see it.
  • Username. So your friends can find you. All registered users can see it.
  • Email address (only accounts created with earlier versions of the app). We no longer ask for it and we do not use it. If you gave it to us, it stays among your private contacts as long as your account exists. Only you see it.
  • Profile photo and bio (optional). To introduce yourself to friends. Registered users can see them. You pick the photo with your phone's photo picker: the app does not access your other photos. It is stored in Firebase Storage.
  • Instagram username (optional). To share it with a friend. It is stored with your phone number, among your private contacts: only you see it. A friend sees it only after a mutual “It's Hora!” and only if you chose to share it.
  • Friends list and friend requests. This is the core of the app. Only you and the friend involved see them. Each person can send at most 30 requests a day.
  • Requests you declined. For 30 days, the person you declined cannot send you a new request. Only our server writes this, among your data. The declined person is not told.
  • “Free” status and times (from / until). To let friends know you're free. You choose when it ends: in 1 hour, in 3 hours or “Tonight” (until 2 am). Only the friends you choose each time you mark yourself free, and only while you are free. Nobody sees who else you chose.
  • The evening link (“Tell people who don't have Hora”, optional). While you are free you can create a link and send it wherever you like, for example to a WhatsApp group. Whoever opens it, even without HORA, sees only your name, until when you are free, your “what you're up for” line if you wrote one, and the weather: not your number, not your Instagram, not your friends. The link stops working when your evening ends or when you switch it off.
  • People who answer “I'm in” from the link (even without HORA). The name they write, to notify whoever sent the link. Only the sender sees it; people who answer do not see the other names. At most 20 answers per link. Against abuse we also keep an encrypted fingerprint of the internet address and browser, never the address itself. The link page uses no cookies and no analytics and asks for nothing else.
  • Last group of friends you picked. To suggest it next time. Only you see it.
  • “Hora?” requests (who asked whom, and when). To share contacts when the wish to meet is mutual (within 12 hours). Only the two people involved see them.
  • Blocked users. To stop the people you block from contacting you. When you block someone, we also remove the phone number and Instagram you had exchanged with them. Only you see this list.
  • Phone numbers in your contacts (only if you use “Find in contacts”). To show you which of your contacts are already on HORA. The app reads your contacts only if you tap “Search” and give permission; on iPhone you can also share just some contacts. Only the numbers reach our server, encrypted: names, emails and the rest of your address book stay on your phone. The server compares them with the numbers of registered users and discards them at once: we do not store them, they do not end up in logs, and we never message your contacts. The numbers of people who do not use HORA also pass through the server only for that moment. To prevent abuse, at most 3 searches and 2,000 numbers per day: we count how many, not which ones.
  • “Findable from contacts”. On by default: someone who has your number in their contacts and uses “Find in contacts” sees that you are on HORA, with your name and username (not your number: they already have it). You can turn it off at any time in Profile → Settings → “Findable from contacts”. People you blocked, or who blocked you, cannot find you anyway. Only you see this setting.
  • Reports. To handle abuse: who reported whom, the reason and the text. Only the people who run HORA see them.
  • Push notification token. To send you notifications. Nobody sees it: only our server uses it.
  • Age confirmation. When you sign up, you confirm you are at least 14. We do not store your answer or your date of birth.
  • App language. To write notifications in your language. Nobody sees it.
  • Anonymous daily counters. To check that the app works, for example how many requests are sent each day. They contain no names and no user identifiers.

HORA does not use your location. HORA reads your contacts only when you use “Find in contacts”, as explained above, and does not access your photo library. There are no ads, no tracking across apps or websites, and no analytics tools inside the app. We do not sell your data.

Legal basis

  • Contract (art. 6.1.b GDPR). Most data is needed to give you the service you asked for when you signed up.
  • Legitimate interest (art. 6.1.f GDPR). Blocks, reports and security checks protect users from abuse. Comparing the numbers in your contacts, including those of people who do not use HORA, helps you find your friends and lasts only a moment. The name of who answers from an evening link is used to notify the sender, and the address fingerprint protects against abuse; both last only as long as the evening. The anonymous daily counters help us keep the app working. You can object to this use (see “Your rights”).

Who processes the data for us

  • Google Firebase (Google Ireland Ltd), as our data processor. It hosts the database, the server functions and the profile photos.
  • Firebase Authentication (Google) sends the SMS login codes and stores your phone number and sign-in data.
  • Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google) delivers push notifications. On iPhone they go through the Apple Push Notification service (Apple).

We share data with no one else, except when the law requires it.

Where the data is stored

The database is in the European Union, in the eur3 region (Europe). The server functions are in europe-west1 (Belgium).

Not everything is in the EU, though:

  • Your phone number and sign-in data are in Firebase Authentication, a global Google service: they may be stored outside the EU.
  • Profile photos are stored in Firebase Storage, today in the United States (us-central1).
  • Google may also process some data outside the EU to deliver SMS or notifications.

In all these cases Google uses the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.

How long we keep it

  • Your data stays as long as your account exists.
  • The phone number and Instagram you shared in an “It's Hora!” stay in that match, visible only to you and your friend, until one of you deletes their account or blocks the other.
  • The evening link and the names of who answered “I'm in” are deleted when the evening ends or is switched off (at the latest within 30 minutes), together with that link's fingerprints. The fingerprint used for the per-address limit on answers is deleted after one hour.
  • Technical server logs (with your account ID, no name) are kept for 30 days.
  • When you delete your account, your data is deleted immediately from our active systems.
  • Backup copies are deleted within 30 days.
  • Reports made by you or about you are kept for 12 months, also after deletion, to handle abuse.
  • Anonymous counters contain no personal data.

Deleting your account

In the app: Profile → Settings → Delete account. You can also write to privacy@hora-app.com. More details on the account deletion page.

Your rights

You can ask to access, correct, delete or export your data (portability). You can ask us to restrict its use, and you can object to the uses based on legitimate interest. Write to privacy@hora-app.com.

You can also complain to the Italian data protection authority, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali (garanteprivacy.it), or to the authority of the EU country where you live.

Automated decisions

We make no decisions about you based only on automated processing, and we do no profiling.

Minors

HORA is not meant for anyone under 14 years old. When you sign up, we ask you to confirm you are at least 14.

Security

Data is stored on Google's cloud infrastructure and encrypted in transit. Access rules make sure each user can read only what they are allowed to see.

Changes

If we change this policy, we will tell you in the app and update the date at the top of this page.
